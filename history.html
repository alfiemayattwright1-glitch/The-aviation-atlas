Chronological Flight History

Tracing milestone breakthroughs from early powered flight pioneers to modern twin-engine widebodies.

December 17, 1903

The Wright Brothers at Kitty Hawk

Orville and Wilbur Wright successfully pilot the Wright Flyer at Kitty Hawk, North Carolina, achieving the first sustained, controlled, powered heavier-than-air flight.

May 20-21, 1927

Charles Lindbergh's Transatlantic Solo

American aviator Charles Lindbergh completes the first solo nonstop transatlantic flight from New York to Paris aboard the Spirit of St. Louis.

May 2, 1952

Dawn of Commercial Jet Aviation

The de Havilland Comet enters scheduled passenger service with BOAC, inaugurating the commercial jet age and cutting travel times in half.

January 21, 1976

Commercial Supersonic Flight

Concorde enters scheduled supersonic service with British Airways and Air France, crossing the Atlantic at Mach 2.04.

Present Day

Modern Twin-Engine Era & Composite Efficiency

Advanced high-bypass turbofans and carbon-composite airframes (Boeing 787, Airbus A350) enable unprecedented ultra-long-haul point-to-point global connectivity.
